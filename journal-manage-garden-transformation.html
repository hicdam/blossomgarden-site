Who should manage a garden transformation

Practical guidance for homeowners across Surrey, Sussex and Hampshire.

A large garden transformation rarely involves just one trade.

There may be a garden designer, landscape contractor, tree surgeon, electrician, irrigation specialist, pool contractor, garden-room supplier, lighting designer and planting team. Somebody needs to keep those parts connected.

Choose who will coordinate the work

The right person depends on the project. A landscape contractor may be able to manage a straightforward build where most of the work sits within their own team. A garden designer may coordinate design-led work through to completion. On more complex schemes, an independent project or garden manager can sit across all suppliers.

Give the lead a clear role

Whoever takes the lead should own the programme, scope, decisions, changes and handovers. They should know what is happening next, what information is missing and whether one contractor's work affects another.

Join up decisions across trades

This becomes especially important around drainage, levels, electrics, foundations, access and planting. A decision made in one area can easily create extra cost somewhere else.

Keep one version of the plan

The person managing the project should also keep track of quotations, variations, payments and quality checks. That does not mean every decision has to be centralised, but there should be one version of the plan.

A transformation becomes expensive when trades work in isolation. Good management is mostly about sequencing, communication and making sure the garden is treated as one project rather than a collection of separate jobs.
