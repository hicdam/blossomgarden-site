How to build a reliable garden supplier network

Practical guidance for homeowners across Surrey, Sussex and Hampshire.

Most larger gardens eventually need more than one supplier.

You may need a maintenance gardener, tree surgeon, landscaper, irrigation engineer, electrician, pond specialist, fencing contractor, garden designer or someone to look after a pool or garden building.

The problem is not finding names. It is knowing who is reliable.

Start with clear scopes of work. Good suppliers are easier to assess when they are being asked to price or deliver something specific.

Keep records of who has worked in the garden, what they did, how they performed and whether you would use them again. Over time, this becomes far more useful than searching from scratch every time something needs attention.

Where possible, use specialists for specialist work. A good general landscaper may be able to coordinate several trades, but that does not mean they should personally handle every technical task.

Availability matters too. If a supplier is critical to the garden, understand how far ahead they normally book and whether they provide emergency support.

Most importantly, avoid relying on one person for everything. A healthy supplier network gives you options, lets you compare advice and reduces the risk of being stuck when somebody is unavailable.

The aim is a small group of trusted people who already understand the garden and can be called on when needed.
