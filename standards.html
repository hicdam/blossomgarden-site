Our standards

Clear rules for how Blossom manages your garden and your money.

A practical operating model for decisions, suppliers, specialists, costs, quality and accountability.

The operating model

A young business can still be explicit about how it behaves.

Blossom does not rely on vague promises of “quality” or “trusted suppliers”. The standards below describe how we intend to make decisions, manage projects and protect the client's position from the first conversation onward.

Client control

You keep the important decisions.

Major design direction, budget, supplier appointments and significant changes remain with you unless you explicitly delegate authority. Routine decisions can be handled within agreed limits.

Commercial clarity

No hidden arrangements.

Fees, procurement charges, supplier margins or commissions are disclosed before you approve the relevant work. Significant cost changes are made visible before commitment wherever reasonably possible.

Supplier selection

Fit before familiarity.

Suppliers are considered against the project: relevant capability, experience, scope clarity, availability, insurance where required, warranty, communication and value. Existing relationships do not remove that test.

Specialist responsibility

The right expert owns the right work.

Structural, electrical, engineering, arboricultural, planning and other specialist work remains the responsibility of the appropriately competent person appointed to provide it. Blossom coordinates; it does not pretend one person can cover every discipline.

Quality

Define, observe, record, resolve.

Blossom sets out the agreed requirement, observes progress against it, records defects or incomplete work and tracks issues to closure. The contractor remains responsible for its workmanship and warranties.

Existing suppliers

Good relationships stay.

If you already have a capable gardener, designer or contractor, Blossom can work with them. We replace suppliers only where capability, availability, fit or performance makes that necessary.

When something goes wrong

No passing the problem around.

We establish the facts, identify who owns the issue, make the impact visible, agree a route to resolution and track it until it is closed or formally handed back to the responsible party.

Sustainability

Avoid unnecessary work first.

Where sensible, we prefer to retain, repair and improve before replacing. Plant choice, water use, soil health, biodiversity, durability and whole-life cost are considered as part of the decision rather than added as decoration later.

Scope and protection

Responsibility is written down.

Before appointment, Blossom defines the role it is taking, what sits outside that role and where specialist responsibility remains with others. Any project-specific insurance or professional requirements are checked as part of that appointment rather than assumed.

Procurement

Comparable information before comparable prices.

Where Blossom obtains quotations, we try to issue a consistent scope, expose assumptions and exclusions, and compare what is actually being offered. The recommendation is based on capability, value and fit, not the lowest headline number.

Budget control

Changes stay visible.

Projects should have a working budget, agreed scope, known exclusions and a visible record of changes. The aim is to avoid surprises and make decisions before cost is committed wherever the project allows it.

These are operating standards, not marketing claims.

They are the basis on which Blossom intends to be appointed, brief specialists and manage client work.
