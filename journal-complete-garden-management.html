What complete garden management means

Practical guidance for homeowners across Surrey, Sussex and Hampshire.

Complete garden management means looking after the whole garden as one system, rather than treating each job as a separate problem.

A gardener may cut the lawn, prune shrubs and keep borders tidy. A designer may reshape the space. A contractor may build a terrace or install drainage. A tree surgeon may deal with mature trees. All of those jobs matter, but they can easily pull in different directions if nobody is looking at the whole picture.

Garden management joins those pieces together. It starts with understanding how the garden is used, what condition it is in, what needs attention now and what can wait. From there, the work can be planned in the right order.

That might mean fixing drainage before replacing a lawn, agreeing a planting plan before changing irrigation, or deciding where a future garden room will sit before investing in new borders around it.

It also means coordinating the people involved. Good garden management should make sure trades are properly briefed, quotes can be compared on the same basis, work happens in the right sequence and problems are dealt with before they become expensive.

For larger gardens, it should also include an annual plan. Seasonal maintenance, tree work, planting, repairs, improvements and larger projects can then be scheduled around the garden rather than dealt with reactively.

The aim is simple: one clear plan for the garden, with the right people doing the right work at the right time.
