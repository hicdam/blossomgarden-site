How to plan your garden jobs for the year

Practical guidance for homeowners across Surrey, Sussex and Hampshire.

A yearly garden jobs plan is simply a clear list of what needs to happen in the garden, when it should happen and who is responsible.

Map the work across the year

Start with the work that repeats every year. Lawn care, hedge cutting, pruning, leaf clearance, irrigation checks, pond care, tree inspections and seasonal planting all have natural windows in the calendar.

Then add one-off work. This might include repairing paving, replacing a fence, improving drainage, redesigning a border, installing lighting or preparing for a larger project.

Book specialists and review points

Next, mark anything that needs a specialist. Tree work, electrics, structural work, irrigation, drainage and major landscaping should not be left until the week they become urgent. Good contractors can be booked well in advance.

It is also worth putting review points into the year. Spring is useful for checking winter damage and new growth. High summer shows whether planting, shade and watering are working. Autumn is the right time to review what should change before the following season.

Keep the plan simple

A simple plan can be kept in a spreadsheet or calendar. For each task, record the timing, expected cost, person responsible and whether it is routine, corrective or an improvement.

The point is not to create paperwork. It is to stop the garden being managed by surprise. Once the year is visible, budgets are easier to control, trades can be booked earlier and work can happen in the right order.
